Math Art Gallery

Every piece on these walls was drawn by an equation. No brushes, no hand tuning of pixels, just a few lines of math iterated millions of times. Click any frame for the full resolution image.

No. 1

Clifford Attractor

x' = sin(a y) + c cos(a x)
y' = sin(b x) + d cos(b y)

a = -1.3, b = -1.3, c = -1.8, d = -1.9, 10 million points

Two lines of trigonometry, iterated ten million times. Each point is computed from the one before it, and the orbit never settles into a repeating loop, yet it always traces the same tangle of ribbons. Darker ink means more visits.

Peter de Jong Attractor

x' = sin(a y) - cos(b x)
y' = sin(c x) - cos(d y)

a = 1.4, b = -2.3, c = 2.4, d = -2.1, 12 million points

A close cousin of the Clifford map with the cosines subtracted instead of added. The same idea, a different personality: it folds into smoke instead of ribbons. Rendered like a long exposure of light on black.

Lorenz Attractor

dx/dt = s (y - x)
dy/dt = x (r - z) - y
dz/dt = x y - b z

s = 10, r = 28, b = 8/3, RK4 with dt = 0.002, 6 trajectories

Edward Lorenz's simplified weather model, the original butterfly. Six particles start a hair apart and are carried along the flow. Within seconds they disagree completely about where they are, but all of them keep circling the same two wings.

Julia Set

z' = z^2 + c
c = -0.8 + 0.156i

500 iterations, smooth escape-time coloring

Every pixel is a starting value of z. Square it, add c, repeat. Points that stay bounded forever form the set; the color outside shows how quickly each point escaped. The edge is infinitely detailed.

Barnsley Fern

pick one of four affine maps at random
(x, y) -> A (x, y) + b

4 maps chosen with probability 1%, 85%, 7%, 7%, 8 million points

A fern drawn by rolling dice. Each map is a simple stretch, rotate and shift; one makes the stem, one makes each leaf a smaller copy of the whole, and two make the first left and right leaflets.

Harmonograph

x(t) = e^(-d1 t) sin(f1 t + p1) + e^(-d2 t) sin(f2 t + p2)
y(t) = e^(-d3 t) sin(f3 t + p3) + e^(-d4 t) sin(f4 t + p4)

A Victorian drawing machine in software: a pen moved by swinging pendulums that slowly lose energy. Frequencies close to a simple ratio, but not quite, make the figure drift and spiral inward.

Maurer Rose

r = sin(n theta)
connect the points at theta = k d degrees, k = 0..360

n = 6, d = 71

Start with the rose curve r = sin(6 theta), then ignore it and walk around it in jumps of 71 degrees, joining each landing spot with a straight line. The rose (in red) is hidden inside its own web.

No. 8

Phyllotaxis

theta_k = k * 137.508 degrees
r_k = sqrt(k)

1,500 seeds, golden angle

How a sunflower packs its seeds. Each new seed is placed one golden angle around from the last. Because the golden ratio is the 'most irrational' number, the seeds never line up into gaps, and the spiral families you see come in Fibonacci numbers.

Buddhabrot

z' = z^2 + c,  z_0 = 0
plot every z along the orbits that escape

12 million samples of c; orbits escaping in 200-1000 steps paint orange, 40-200 green, 8-40 blue

The Mandelbrot set turned inside out. Instead of coloring each c by whether it escapes, I follow the points that do escape and plot every place their orbit visits on the way out. Slow escapes paint orange, quick ones blue. Turned on its side, a seated figure appears.

Aizawa Attractor

dx/dt = (z - b) x - d y
dy/dt = d x + (z - b) y
dz/dt = c + a z - z^3/3 - (x^2 + y^2)(1 + e z) + f z x^3

a = 0.95, b = 0.7, c = 0.6, d = 3.5, e = 0.25, f = 0.1, 300 particles

Particles spiral around a sphere-like shell, get pulled up a narrow tube through the middle, and spill back over the top. Seen from the side and slightly above, the paths look like a spun-glass apple.

No. 11

Newton Fractal

z' = z - p(z) / p'(z)
p(z) = z^3 - 1

60 iterations, colored by which root each point reaches

Newton's method finds roots by sliding down tangent lines. Start it everywhere on the complex plane and color each point by which of the three cube roots of 1 it lands on. Between any two colors, the third always squeezes in, forever.

No. 12

Ikeda Map

t = 0.4 - 6 / (1 + x^2 + y^2)
x' = 1 + u (x cos t - y sin t)
y' = u (x sin t + y cos t)

u = 0.9, 10 million points

A model of light circling a ring-shaped laser cavity, losing a little energy and picking up a twist on every lap. The twist depends on how far from the center the light is, which is enough to fold the orbit into this curling wave.

No. 13

Gray-Scott Reaction-Diffusion

du/dt = Du lap(u) - u v^2 + F (1 - u)
dv/dt = Dv lap(v) + u v^2 - (F + k) v

Du = 0.16, Dv = 0.08, F = 0.029, k = 0.057, 10,000 steps on a 300 x 300 grid

Two imaginary chemicals: one is fed in, the other eats it and reproduces. Both spread out, at different speeds. Starting from a few random specks, that balance grows into the same maze-like stripes you see on fish, coral and fingerprints.

Hopalong

No. 15

Chaos Game

pick a random corner of a pentagon (never the same one twice in a row)
p' = p + (corner - p) / 2

5 corners, jump ratio 1/2, 10 million points

Pick a corner of a pentagon at random and move halfway toward it. Repeat. With no rules the points fill the shape with mush, but one small restriction (don't pick the same corner twice in a row) is enough to carve out this lace. Each point takes the color of its last corner.

No. 16

Abelian Sandpile

drop N grains on one cell
any cell with 4 or more grains gives 1 to each neighbor

N = 2^16 grains, colored by 0, 1, 2 or 3 grains left

Pour sand onto a single square of a grid. Whenever a square holds four grains it topples, passing one to each neighbor, which may topple in turn. When everything finally settles, this fractal of nested triangles and ovals is what's left. The order of topplings doesn't matter; the result is always identical.
