No. 12

Ikeda Map

Strange attractor, 2026. Python and numpy.
t = 0.4 - 6 / (1 + x^2 + y^2)
x' = 1 + u (x cos t - y sin t)
y' = u (x sin t + y cos t)

A model of light circling a ring-shaped laser cavity, losing a little energy and picking up a twist on every lap. The twist depends on how far from the center the light is, which is enough to fold the orbit into this curling wave.

Kensuke Ikeda proposed this map in 1979 to describe light travelling around a ring cavity that contains a nonlinear material, the kind of setup found inside some lasers. Each step is one trip around the ring: the light loses some energy (the factor u) and its phase shifts by an amount that depends on its intensity.

At u = 0.9 the attractor shares the plane with a stable fixed point, and some starting points spiral into that instead. For this render, any point that has stopped moving after the warm-up is thrown away, leaving only the curling attractor.

u = 0.9, 10 million points
