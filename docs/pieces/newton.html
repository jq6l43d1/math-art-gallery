No. 11

Newton Fractal

Basins of attraction, 2026. Python and numpy.
z' = z - p(z) / p'(z)
p(z) = z^3 - 1

Newton's method finds roots by sliding down tangent lines. Start it everywhere on the complex plane and color each point by which of the three cube roots of 1 it lands on. Between any two colors, the third always squeezes in, forever.

Newton's method is a 17th century technique for finding where a function equals zero: make a guess, follow the tangent line down to where it crosses zero, and repeat. For most starting points it homes in quickly. In 1879 Arthur Cayley asked which root each starting point in the complex plane would reach, and found the question surprisingly hard for cubics.

This picture is the answer. Each color is one root of z^3 - 1, and the shading shows how many steps it took to get there. The boundaries are fractal: anywhere two colors meet, the third is also present, no matter how far you zoom in.
