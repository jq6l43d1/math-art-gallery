No. 5

Barnsley Fern

Iterated function system, 2026. Python and numpy.
pick one of four affine maps at random
(x, y) -> A (x, y) + b

A fern drawn by rolling dice. Each map is a simple stretch, rotate and shift; one makes the stem, one makes each leaf a smaller copy of the whole, and two make the first left and right leaflets.

Michael Barnsley used this fern in his 1988 book Fractals Everywhere to show how little information a natural-looking shape needs. The whole plant is encoded in 28 numbers: six coefficients and one probability for each of four maps.

The probabilities only affect how evenly the fern fills in; the shape itself is fixed by the maps. The render runs 200,000 points at once, each choosing its own map at every step, and the brightness of each pixel comes from how often it was landed on.

4 maps chosen with probability 1%, 85%, 7%, 7%, 8 million points
