No. 14

Hopalong

Orbit of a map, 2026. Python and numpy.
x' = y - sign(x) sqrt(|b x - c|)
y' = a - x

One point hops around the plane four million times. It never repeats, but it keeps returning to the same family of tiles, and coloring it by time shows the pattern slowly growing outward.

Barry Martin of Aston University devised this map, and A. K. Dewdney featured it in his Computer Recreations column in Scientific American in 1986 under the name Hopalong.

Unlike the attractors elsewhere in the gallery, the picture depends on where the point starts, so this is a single orbit computed four million times in a plain loop. The orbit is cut into six equal stretches of time, each drawn in its own color, so you can see how the pattern spreads as the point keeps hopping.

a = 1.1, b = 0.5, c = 1, 4 million steps from (0, 0), colored by time
