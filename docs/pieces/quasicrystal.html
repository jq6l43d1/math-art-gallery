No. 18

Quasicrystal

Wave interference, 2026. Python and numpy.
f(x, y) = sum over k = 0..6 of cos(x cos(k pi/7) + y sin(k pi/7))

Seven flat ripples crossing at equal angles. Two, three, four or six waves would make a repeating tiling, but seven can't: the pattern has perfect sevenfold symmetry around the center and yet never repeats anywhere, like the quasicrystals that won the 2011 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

In 1982 Dan Shechtman looked at an aluminium-manganese alloy under an electron microscope and saw a symmetry that crystallographers believed was impossible. Materials like it are ordered but never periodic, and for years many scientists refused to accept them.

This image is a sum of seven cosine waves, each tilted one seventh of a half turn from the last. Anywhere you look you can find patches that nearly repeat, but no shift maps the whole pattern exactly onto itself. Passing the sum through a sine turns both peaks and troughs into bright bands.
