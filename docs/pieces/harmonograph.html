No. 6

Harmonograph

Damped oscillation, 2026. Python and numpy.
x(t) = e^(-d1 t) sin(f1 t + p1) + e^(-d2 t) sin(f2 t + p2)
y(t) = e^(-d3 t) sin(f3 t + p3) + e^(-d4 t) sin(f4 t + p4)

A Victorian drawing machine in software: a pen moved by swinging pendulums that slowly lose energy. Frequencies close to a simple ratio, but not quite, make the figure drift and spiral inward.

Harmonographs were a popular parlor device in the Victorian era: a table with pendulums underneath, some swinging a pen and others swinging the paper. Friction slowly drains the swing, so the figure shrinks toward the center as it is drawn.

Here each axis is the sum of two damped sine waves. Frequencies of 3 and 2 alone would trace a stable figure over and over. Detuning one of them to 2.006 makes the figure rotate a little on every pass, which turns a single shape into layered petals. Six million points along the curve are counted into pixels, so overlapping strokes darken like real ink.

f = 3, 2, 3, 2.006, damping around 0.005 per second
