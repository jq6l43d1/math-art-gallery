No. 13

Gray-Scott Reaction-Diffusion

Reaction-diffusion, 2026. Python and numpy.
du/dt = Du lap(u) - u v^2 + F (1 - u)
dv/dt = Dv lap(v) + u v^2 - (F + k) v

Two imaginary chemicals: one is fed in, the other eats it and reproduces. Both spread out, at different speeds. Starting from a few random specks, that balance grows into the same maze-like stripes you see on fish, coral and fingerprints.

Alan Turing suggested in 1952 that two chemicals reacting and spreading at different speeds could produce the spots and stripes seen on animals. The Gray-Scott model, from chemists Peter Gray and Stephen Scott in the 1980s, is one of the simplest systems that does it.

Two numbers, the feed rate F and the kill rate k, pick the pattern. Small changes give spots that divide like cells, travelling waves, or, as here, a labyrinth of stripes. The simulation runs 10,000 steps on a 300 by 300 grid and is scaled up smoothly for display.

Du = 0.16, Dv = 0.08, F = 0.029, k = 0.057, 10,000 steps on a 300 x 300 grid
