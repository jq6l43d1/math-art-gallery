r_k = sqrt(k)

How a sunflower packs its seeds. Each new seed is placed one golden angle around from the last. Because the golden ratio is the 'most irrational' number, the seeds never line up into gaps, and the spiral families you see come in Fibonacci numbers.

The golden angle, about 137.5 degrees, splits a full turn in the golden ratio. In 1979 Helmut Vogel showed that placing seeds one golden angle apart, with the radius growing like the square root of the seed number, reproduces the head of a sunflower.

Count the spirals curving one way and then the other and you get neighboring Fibonacci numbers, such as 34 and 55. Change the angle by a fraction of a degree and the seeds collapse into straight spokes with gaps between them. The color runs from the first seed at the center to the last one at the rim.
